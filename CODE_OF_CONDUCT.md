# Code of Conduct

PrivateLabBench is a scientific open-source project. We want discussions and contributions to be rigorous, useful, and welcoming to people with different backgrounds and levels of experience.

## Expected behavior

Participants should:

- treat other contributors with respect;
- critique ideas, code, experiments, and claims rather than people;
- be specific and evidence-driven when disagreeing;
- acknowledge uncertainty and correct mistakes openly;
- give credit for ideas and prior work;
- respect privacy, security, licensing, and data-use constraints;
- help keep issue and review discussions on topic;
- make space for newcomers to ask reasonable questions.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or sustained hostile behavior;
- discriminatory or demeaning comments about protected/personal characteristics;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- deliberately exposing credentials, private datasets, vulnerability details, or other sensitive material;
- personal attacks or repeated bad-faith disruption;
- retaliation against someone who reports a conduct or security concern.

Technical disagreement, strong review feedback, and rejection of a proposed change are not harassment when they are focused on the work and communicated professionally.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, code review, project discussions, and other spaces where someone is representing PrivateLabBench or its community.

## Reporting conduct concerns

For ordinary disagreements, first try to resolve the problem in the relevant issue or review thread without escalating the tone.

For harassment, threats, doxxing, or other sensitive conduct concerns, contact the repository maintainer privately through their GitHub profile rather than posting sensitive details publicly. If GitHub's reporting/moderation tools apply, they may also be used.

Security vulnerabilities should follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit/remove content, lock threads, warn participants, restrict participation, or reject contributions when needed to protect the community and project.

Enforcement decisions should be proportional, documented when appropriate, and focused on keeping the project safe and productive rather than winning a disagreement.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers are held to the same standard. A maintainer with a personal conflict in a conduct dispute should, when possible, ask another trusted maintainer or neutral reviewer to help evaluate the situation.
